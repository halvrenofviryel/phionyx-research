 Decision Receipts for Approvals, Declined Actions and Executed Actions
                         in Agent Audit Records
                draft-gruszka-audit-decision-receipts-04

Abstract

   The architecture for auditing agent delegation and interactions
   proposed for the AUDIT effort names Interaction Records, Action
   Records and Authorization Transition Records, and leaves their
   encoding open.  This document shows how an existing signed record,
   the decision receipt of the open-source proofbundle project, together
   with its companion outcome receipt, gives three such records exact
   bytes, a signature and digest bindings that a third party can check
   offline.  The three cases are a human approval, a declined action and
   an executed action with its result.  For each case the document maps
   the receipt members onto the record types of the architecture, states
   what the receipt adds and what it does not show, and gives an offline
   verification procedure.  It states one requirement for approvals,
   that an approval is honoured at most once, also after the record of
   consumed approvals is rotated.  Test vectors for all three cases,
   each with a counterexample, are included.  The document also
   describes, informatively, how a receipt can be registered with a
   Transparency Service under RFC 9943 and what a SCITT Receipt then
   shows and does not show.  This document defines no new format,
   predicate or registry.

1.  Introduction

   The architecture of [AUDIT-ARCH] asks that auditing link user intent,
   delegation, authorization and execution, and treats authorization as
   a state that changes over time.  Three of its four record types (its
   Section 5) matter here.  Interaction Records capture user-facing
   events such as approvals and refusals, Action Records capture events
   at the boundary where an action took effect, and Authorization
   Transition Records capture changes in permission state.  Its field
   names are placeholders (its Section 7), and a canonical data model is
   a work item (WI-1).

   An Auditor in that architecture does not trust the Agent (its
   Section 8), so a record helps only when a third party can check it.
   That needs exact bytes, a signature over them and bindings to inputs
   and evidence by digest.  This document shows that the decision
   receipt of proofbundle [PB-DECISION], an in-toto Statement
   [INTOTO-STATEMENT] in a DSSE envelope [DSSE], signed with Ed25519
   [RFC8032] over a payload in the serialization of [RFC8785], supplies
   them together with its companion outcome receipt [PB-OUTCOME].  It
   does so for three cases that Agent Guard, the gate of the proofbundle
   plugin for two coding agent hosts, produces (Section 5):

   1.  a human approval of one action (an Interaction Record of an
       approval and an Authorization Transition Record with the trigger
       user_approval);

   2.  a declined action, with its reason and without an effect;

   3.  an executed action with its result (an Action Record).

   The document maps and verifies.  It does not propose the decision
   receipt as the data model of the architecture and does not change the
   receipts it describes.  The mapping of Section 2 follows revision -01
   of [AUDIT-ARCH]; a later revision of the architecture can change it.

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in

   capitals, as shown here.

   In this document, these key words apply to a verifier in Section 2.6
   and in the procedure of Section 3, to an Auditor that performs the
   checks of Section 3.5, to a relying party that lets an action run
   because of an approval in Section 4, and to the use of the test keys
   of Appendix A.

1.2.  Terms

   Decision receipt, outcome receipt:  A DSSE envelope whose payload is
      an in-toto Statement v1 with the predicate type
      https://b7n0de.com/proofbundle/predicates/decision-receipt/v0.1 or
      https://b7n0de.com/proofbundle/predicates/action-outcome/v0.1.
   Payload bytes:  The bytes obtained by base64 decoding the payload
      member of the envelope.  A DSSE signature covers the pre-
      authentication encoding of payloadType and these bytes.
   Content root:  SHA-256 [RFC6234] over the payload bytes.  Other
      digests commit to the byte strings identified by their respective
      fields.
   Approval record:  The record of one human act that approves one
      action (Section 2.1).
   Relying party:  The component that lets an action run because of a
      decision.
   Consumption record:  The durable record in which a relying party
      notes each approval it has honoured.

2.  Mapping the Three Cases onto AUDIT Records

   All decision receipts here use the decisionType
   preActionAuthorization and carry validity with audience, nonce and
   expiresAt.  A decision receipt is the gate's signed view of an event;
   the human act itself is the approval record, bound by digest.  In the
   tables, IR stands for Interaction Record, ATR for Authorization
   Transition Record and AR for Action Record, followed by a placeholder
   field of the architecture.  The fields common to all cases map as
   follows.

   event_id                  decisionId (outcomeId in an outcome
                             receipt)
   trace_id, parent_id       traceContext.traceId, traceContext.parentId
   timestamp                 decidedAt (performedAt in an outcome

2.1.  Case 1, a Human Approval

   A gate asks a person before an action and records the answer.  An
   earlier receipt for the same request, such as one with the verdict
   ESCALATE, is a separate record; this receipt does not reference it.
   After the answer the gate decides ALLOW for exactly the approved
   action.  The approval record carries approvalId, approver, session,
   actionDigest (SHA-256 of the approved action bytes), approvedAt and
   validUntil.  The decision receipt names it in inputSnapshot under the
   name "approval record".

   +================+=================================================+
   | Record, field  | Decision receipt                                |
   +================+=================================================+
   | IR, approval   | approval record, by its digest in inputSnapshot |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------------+
   | IR, actor      | approver in the approval record, equal to       |
   |                | principal.id                                    |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------------+
   | ATR,           | not recorded in this receipt; preceding state   |
   | previous_state | must be established separately                  |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------------+
   | ATR, new_state | verdict ALLOW for parametersDigest              |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------------+
   | ATR, trigger   | reasonCodes contains user_approval              |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------------+
   | ATR, actor     | approver in the approval record                 |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------------+

                             Table 1: Case 1

   The scope of the new state is one action, identified by its bytes.  A
   second action, even one with the same name and target, needs a new
   approval.

2.2.  Case 2, a Declined Action

   A gate declines an action because a rule or a person says no.  The
   receipt records the verdict DENY with its reasons.  It may say in
   actionOutcome that the call was blocked, which is the decision
   maker's own statement.

         +=========+============================================+
         | Record, | Decision receipt                           |
         | field   |                                            |
         +=========+============================================+
         | IR,     | verdict DENY or REFUSE                     |
         | refusal |                                            |
         +---------+--------------------------------------------+
         | reason  | reasonCodes                                |
         +---------+--------------------------------------------+
         | action  | proposedAction with parametersDigest       |
         +---------+--------------------------------------------+
         | no      | actionOutcome.status blocked (self-        |
         | effect  | asserted); no outcome with status executed |
         |         | names this decision in the supplied set    |
         +---------+--------------------------------------------+
         | ATR     | none; the state is unchanged               |
         +---------+--------------------------------------------+

                             Table 2: Case 2

   A receipt cannot prove that something did not happen.  An observer at
   the boundary can separately report that it observed no effect.  The
   check in Section 3.5 detects a conflicting executed outcome in the
   supplied set; it does not establish absence of an effect.

   A gate allows an action, the action runs, and an executor or an
   observer at the boundary records what happened in an outcome receipt
   signed with its own key.  The outcome receipt names the decision by
   its content root in decisionRef.

   The architecture asks that Action Records be produced at the boundary
   where the action took effect.  Its WI-1-3 distinguishes the
   Recorder's signing identity from the recorded Agent's identity where
   they are operationally separate.  Section 3.4 checks that the outcome
   and decision use different keys and that executor.id differs from
   decisionMaker.id.  Those checks alone do not establish operational
   separation or that the outcome signer observed the boundary.

2.4.  What the Receipt Adds

   *  Exact bytes.  The payload is its own serialization under
      [RFC8785].  A verifier uses the received payload bytes for the
      signature input and content root.  Canonical serialization is used
      to check equality and to derive the predicate's subject digest,
      never to repair or replace the received payload.

   *  A signature.  Ed25519 over the DSSE pre-authentication encoding of
      the payload type and the payload bytes.








Gruszka                    Expires 6 May 2027                   [Page 7]

Internet-Draft           AUDIT Decision Receipts           November 2026


   *  Binding by digest.  The action bytes (parametersDigest), the
      approval record and other inputs (inputSnapshot), the policy
      (policyBoundary.policyDigest), evidence (evidenceRefs), the
      decision itself (decisionRef in the outcome) and the observed
      effect (effectDigest) are named by SHA-256.

   *  Stated limits.  The member notChecked lists what the decision
      maker did not check, and decisionChangeConditions lists what would
      change the decision.

2.5.  What the Receipt Does Not Do

   *  It does not show that a decision was correct, safe or lawful.

   *  It does not authenticate the approval.  The approval record in
      this document is not signed by the person it names.  A deployment
      can additionally cite a separately signed approval in
      inputSnapshot.  Validating that signature and establishing its
      correspondence to the approval record require rules beyond this
      document.  The signed approvals of [IZMAYLOV] are one possible
      source of such evidence.

   *  It does not show that an effect happened.  An outcome receipt
      shows that its signer stated an effect with a given digest.  A
      digest that cannot be resolved to bytes shows only that a value
      was written.

   *  It does not register anything.  Transparency in the sense of
      [RFC9943] needs a separate registration and a SCITT Receipt;
      Section 3.7 describes how a receipt of this document can be
      registered.

   *  It does not detect a receipt that was never written, unless an
      observer or a sequence counter is added.

   *  Its times are the signer's clock.

   *  It is not an access token.  A verified ALLOW is a record of a
      decision, and the executing system makes its own authorization
      check.

2.6.  Evaluation Receipts Are Evidence, Never Authorization

   A decision may rest on an evaluation result, such as a test suite
   that passed.  A Signed Evaluation Receipt [SER] records such a
   result.  It appears in a decision receipt only as an entry of
   evidenceRefs whose digest is the receipt digest, SHA-256 over its
   payload bytes, as in [SER-MAPPINGS].  A verifier MUST NOT treat a
   valid Signed Evaluation Receipt, or its presence in evidenceRefs, as
   permission for an action.  In the records of this document only a
   decision receipt with the verdict ALLOW, from a key the relying party
   trusts for decisions, records a permission.

2.7.  Observations for the Architecture

   This section is informative.  At the places below, the mapping of
   this section needed a reading of revision -01 of [AUDIT-ARCH].  They
   are offered as observations and questions, not as requirements.

   *  Evidence for a human approval.  Section 3 of [AUDIT-ARCH]
      associates Interaction Records with the User; Section 3.1 says
      that user-facing systems produce records of prompts and approvals,
      and Section 4.2 has the Auditing Service sign records under its
      own identity.  In Case 1, the gate's decision receipt binds an
      approval record that names a person but is not signed by that
      person (Section 2.1 and Section 2.5).  What evidence is needed to
      bind such a record to that person's act of approval, and when is
      the person's own signature required?

   *  Previous state.  The Authorization Transition Record of
      Section 7.4 of [AUDIT-ARCH], and its work item WI-1-4 (Section 6
      of [AUDIT-ARCH]), carries a previous state, a new state, a trigger
      and an actor in one record.  A decision receipt records only the
      new state for one action and leaves the previous state to other
      records (Section 2.1).  Section 5 of [AUDIT-ARCH] reconstructs
      authorization from the ordered sequence of such records; is a
      record without the previous state enough for that?

   *  Binding an Action Record to its authorization.  In Section 7.2 of
      [AUDIT-ARCH] the parent_id of an Action Record references the
      Interaction Record that motivated the action, and the
      authorization appears as scope and expiry.  The outcome receipt of
      Case 3 names the decision receipt by its content root in
      decisionRef and takes parent_id from that decision (Section 2.3).
      The examples of [AUDIT-ARCH] have no member that binds an Action
      Record to the authorizing decision by digest.






Gruszka                    Expires 6 May 2027                   [Page 9]

Internet-Draft           AUDIT Decision Receipts           November 2026


   *  Refusals before execution.  Section 5 of [AUDIT-ARCH] includes
      model responses and agent-to-agent dialogue among Interaction
      Records.  Its work item WI-1-2 (Section 6 of [AUDIT-ARCH]) also
      includes system events and does not preclude profiles for non-
      conversational interactions.  In Case 2, a gate can decline a
      proposed action under a rule before execution (Section 2.2).  When
      such a denial is internal to a workflow and is not part of a
      conversation, is it an Interaction Record, an Action Record of an
      attempted request, or a different event type?

   *  Who records an action.  Section 3 of [AUDIT-ARCH] has Action
      Records generated by the Agent, while Section 5 of [AUDIT-ARCH]
      places them at the boundary where the action took effect.  Its
      work item WI-1-3 (Section 6 of [AUDIT-ARCH]) already distinguishes
      the Recorder's signing identity from the recorded Agent's identity
      where they are operationally separated.  In Case 3 the outcome
      signer can be an observer that attests the executor's identifier
      (Section 3.1 and Section 3.4).  How should an Action Record and
      the Auditor's trust policy identify the Recorder separately from
      the actor whose action is recorded?

3.  Offline Verification

   The procedure below needs no network access.  Each step either passes
   or ends the procedure with a failure named by the step.  A later step
   never repairs an earlier failure.

3.1.  Inputs

   The verifier needs the envelope bytes of each receipt and the Ed25519
   public keys selected by its trust policy for the decision signer and,
   for Case 3, the outcome signer.  It also needs the expected
   decisionMaker.id and, for Case 3, the expected executor.id whose
   assertion that policy permits under the outcome-signing key.  An
   outcome signer may be an observer rather than the executor.

   The verifier supplies an evaluation time T in UTC; it does not take T
   from a receipt.  Expected audience and nonce and the action and
   effect byte strings are optional inputs.  Case 1 requires the
   approval record bytes.  Inputs or evidence whose bytes are not
   checked remain digest references, not verified contents.

3.2.  Decision Receipt

   1.   Envelope.  The envelope MUST be valid UTF-8 and parse as one
        JSON object with no duplicate member names in any object.  It
        MUST contain payload, payloadType and signatures. payloadType
        MUST be application/vnd.in-toto+json.



Gruszka                    Expires 6 May 2027                  [Page 10]

Internet-Draft           AUDIT Decision Receipts           November 2026


   2.   Payload bytes.  Decode payload as base64 [RFC4648] into the
        payload bytes P.

   3.   Signature.  Form PAE = "DSSEv1" SP LEN(type) SP type SP LEN(P)
        SP P, where SP is one space, LEN is the length in bytes as ASCII
        decimal without leading zeros, and type is the payloadType.  At
        least one entry of signatures MUST carry a base64 sig that
        verifies as an Ed25519 signature over PAE under the expected
        key.  The keyid member is not used to select a key.

   4.   Canonical form.  P MUST be valid UTF-8 and parse as one JSON
        value with no duplicate member names.  The serialization of the
        parsed value under [RFC8785] MUST be byte for byte equal to P.
        A verifier MUST NOT continue with a re-serialized form.

   5.   Content root.  Compute R = SHA-256(P).

   6.   Statement. _type MUST be https://in-toto.io/Statement/v1 and
        predicateType the decision receipt type of Section 1.2. subject
        MUST have one entry whose digest.sha256 is SHA-256 over the
        [RFC8785] serialization of predicate.

   7.   Members. predicate MUST be a JSON object satisfying the field
        rules of Section 3.8.  It MUST contain schemaVersion,
        decisionId, decisionType, decidedAt, decisionMaker, agent,
        principal, proposedAction, inputSnapshot, policyBoundary (with
        policyDigest), evidenceRefs, decision, notChecked,
        decisionChangeConditions, privacy and validity.  Other predicate
        members are limited to recordedAt, delegationRefs, actionOutcome
        and traceContext.

        decisionMaker, agent, principal, proposedAction, policyBoundary,
        decision, privacy and validity MUST be objects. decisionId,
        decisionMaker.id, agent.id and principal.id MUST be non-empty
        strings. inputSnapshot, evidenceRefs, notChecked and
        decisionChangeConditions MUST be arrays. decision.reasonCodes
        MUST be a non-empty array of non-empty strings.

        schemaVersion MUST consist of "0.1." followed by one or more
        ASCII decimal digits. decisionType MUST be
        preActionAuthorization.  Every timestamp, when non-null, MUST be
        an [RFC3339] UTC timestamp with a trailing "Z".  Every non-null
        digest MUST be an object with exactly one member, sha256, whose
        value is 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits.  A null is allowed
        only where the applicable field rules or Section 3.3 explicitly
        permit it.





Gruszka                    Expires 6 May 2027                  [Page 11]

        The digests of a decision receipt are the members listed below,
        each with its full path in predicate, where [] stands for each
        entry of an array.

        *  agent.configurationDigest
        *  principal.authnContextRef.digest
        *  proposedAction.target.digest
        *  proposedAction.parametersDigest
        *  proposedAction.parametersSchemaRef.digest
        *  inputSnapshot[].digest
        *  policyBoundary.policyDigest
        *  evidenceRefs[].digest
        *  evidenceRefs[].artifactDigest
        *  actionOutcome.outcomeRef.digest

        The timestamps of a decision receipt are the members listed
        below.

        *  decidedAt
        *  recordedAt
        *  policyBoundary.validFrom
        *  policyBoundary.validUntil
        *  actionOutcome.performedAt
        *  validity.expiresAt

        actionOutcome.outcomeRef is an object whose member digest is a
        digest; it is not itself a digest. evidenceRefs[].typedDigest,
        where present, is not a digest in this sense either.  It MUST be
        an object whose members are type, digestAlgorithm and digest and
        optionally purpose; type and purpose MUST be non-empty strings,
        digestAlgorithm MUST be jcs-sha256-v1, and digest MUST be 64
        lowercase hexadecimal digits.

   8.   Signer. decisionMaker.id MUST be the identifier the trust policy
        binds to the key of step 3.

   9.   Validity. validity MUST carry a non-empty array of non-empty
        audience strings, a non-empty nonce string and an expiresAt
        timestamp. decidedAt MUST NOT be later than T, and T MUST be
        strictly earlier than expiresAt.  Where an expected audience is
        given, audience MUST contain it; where an expected nonce is
        given, nonce MUST equal it.

   10.  Action bytes.  Where the action bytes are given, their SHA-256

3.3.  Checks per Case

   Case 1, approval.
      decision.verdict MUST be ALLOW and decision.reasonCodes MUST
      contain user_approval. inputSnapshot MUST contain exactly one
      entry whose name is "approval record"; it may contain other
      entries.  The approval record bytes A MUST be supplied, MUST hash
      to that entry's digest, and MUST be their own [RFC8785]
      serialization.

      A MUST be a JSON object containing approvalId, approver, session,
      actionDigest, approvedAt and validUntil. approvalId, approver and
      session MUST be non-empty strings. actionDigest MUST have the
      digest form of Section 3.2 and MUST equal
      proposedAction.parametersDigest. approver MUST equal principal.id.
      approvedAt and validUntil MUST be [RFC3339] UTC timestamps with a
      trailing "Z".  The bounds approvedAt <= decidedAt <= T <
      validity.expiresAt <= validUntil MUST hold.

   Case 2, declined.
      decision.verdict MUST be DENY or REFUSE.  Where actionOutcome is
      present, its status MUST be notAttempted, blocked or refused, and
      performedAt and outcomeRef MUST be absent or null.

   Case 3, executed.
      decision.verdict MUST be ALLOW.  The outcome receipt MUST have
      status executed and MUST pass Section 3.4 against this decision.

3.5.  Checks over a Set of Receipts

   An Auditor that holds the receipts and approval records of a run can
   check two properties that no single receipt shows.  The Auditor MUST
   obtain the relying party's consumption scope from its audit context
   or trust policy; that scope is not inferred from an approvalId alone.
   First, within that scope, no two distinct ALLOW decision payloads,
   identified by different content roots, may cite approval records with
   the same identity (approvalId, actionDigest and session).  Such a
   pair violates Section 4; additional copies or signatures of one
   payload do not.

   Second, no outcome receipt with status executed may name, in
   decisionRef, the content root of a receipt with the verdict DENY or
   REFUSE.  Both checks are only as complete as the set of receipts the
   Auditor holds.  The first check does not detect two executions that
   reuse one ALLOW receipt without producing another decision receipt.

3.6.  What a Pass Shows

   A pass shows that the holder of the decision maker's key signed
   exactly these payload bytes.  Where action bytes are supplied under
   Section 3.1, their SHA-256 matches
   proposedAction.parametersDigest.sha256.  In Case 1, the SHA-256 of
   the required approval record bytes matches the digest.sha256 value of
   the unique inputSnapshot entry whose name is "approval record".  For
   Case 3, a pass also shows that the holder of a different key, which
   the trust policy permits to attest the expected executor.id, signed
   an outcome with status executed bound to that decision.  It does not
   show that the signer's statements are true, that the approval came
   from the named person, that the outcome signer observed the boundary,
   that no other receipt exists, or that the keys were not misused.
   Those questions belong to the trust policy, to signed approvals, to
   observers and to transparency registration (Section 3.7).

3.7.  Registering a Receipt with a Transparency Service

   This section is informative.  It describes how the receipts of this
   document can be registered with a Transparency Service under
   [RFC9943].  It defines no format, predicate type, media type or
   registry and adds no requirement to those of [RFC9943].

   Registration.  In SCITT a Signed Statement is a COSE_Sign1 message
   whose protected header carries CWT Claims with an Issuer Claim and a
   Subject Claim (Section 6 of [RFC9943]).  A decision receipt or an
   outcome receipt can be registered in two ways.  Its envelope bytes
   can be the payload of a Signed Statement.  Or, as Section 6.2 of
   [RFC9943] allows for Statements too large or too sensitive to send to



Gruszka                    Expires 6 May 2027                  [Page 15]

Internet-Draft           AUDIT Decision Receipts           November 2026


   a Transparency Service, the Signed Statement can be made over a hash,
   here the content root of the receipt.  This document does not define
   how a Signed Statement indicates the hash, its algorithm or its
   content type; that belongs to a deployment's profile and to the
   Registration Policy of the Transparency Service (Section 5.1.1 of
   [RFC9943]).

   Appendix A.4 gives one example Signed Statement for each of the two
   ways, with a SCITT Receipt for each.

   Issuer.  The Issuer of the Signed Statement is the entity identified
   by its iss Claim, whose key signs the COSE_Sign1 (Sections 3 and 6 of
   [RFC9943]).  It can be the decision maker or the outcome signer, or
   another party such as the Auditing Service of Section 4.2 of
   [AUDIT-ARCH].  The Client that registers a Signed Statement need not
   be its Issuer (Section 7 of [RFC9943]).  The Issuer's COSE signature
   is separate from the DSSE signature of the receipt.  The COSE
   signature authenticates the Signed Statement's protected header and
   payload.  The DSSE signature covers the pre-authentication encoding
   of the receipt's payloadType and decoded payload bytes, not the DSSE
   envelope as a whole.  Section 3.2 or Section 3.4 checks that DSSE
   signature and its signer.

   What a SCITT Receipt shows.  During Registration the Transparency
   Service verifies the Issuer's signature and applies its Registration
   Policy (Sections 5.1.1.1 and 6.3 of [RFC9943]).  It registers the
   Signed Statement before it releases a Receipt (Section 6.3 of
   [RFC9943]), and the Receipt demonstrates inclusion of the Signed
   Statement in the Verifiable Data Structure of the Transparency
   Service and can be verified without online access to it (Section 4 of
   [RFC9943]).  The Transparency Service records the Registration time
   when it adds the Signed Statement (Section 7 of [RFC9943]).

   What it does not show.  Registering a Statement proves only that it
   was produced by an Issuer, not that it is accurate (Section 9.2 of
   [RFC9943]).  Issuers can register some Statements and not others
   (Section 9.3 of [RFC9943]), so a Receipt for one receipt says nothing
   about a receipt that was never registered.  The Subject Claim lets
   Relying Parties identify the Transparent Statements registered for a
   Subject (Section 3 of [RFC9943]), not receipts that were never
   registered.  Unless the Registration Policy says otherwise, the order
   of the Verifiable Data Structure need not be the order of issuance
   (Section 9.1 of [RFC9943]).  A Receipt does not change what the
   checks of Section 3 show, and it does not make an ALLOW an
   authorization.






Gruszka                    Expires 6 May 2027                  [Page 16]

Internet-Draft           AUDIT Decision Receipts           November 2026


   Verification.  A verifier checks the SCITT Receipt for the particular
   Signed Statement, including its inclusion proof, using the
   verification key of a Receipt Issuer it trusts and the verification
   rules for the applicable Verifiable Data Structure (Sections 7 and
   7.1 of [RFC9943]).  It establishes the binding from the registered
   Signed Statement to the envelope bytes or content root used in the
   following checks.  If the Signed Statement's COSE payload is detached
   and this binding depends on the Issuer's signature, it verifies that
   signature.  It then applies Section 3 of this document to the
   receipt.  Where the envelope bytes are the Signed Statement's
   payload, it checks those bytes; where the Signed Statement is made
   over the content root, it checks an envelope whose computed content
   root equals the registered value.  This latter comparison binds the
   decoded DSSE payload, not the envelope bytes or the DSSE signature,
   and does not establish that the DSSE signature existed when the
   content root was registered.  Section 7.1 of [RFC9943] permits
   verification of a Receipt without rechecking the Issuer's signature
   on the Signed Statement; this does not dispense with the binding just
   described.  The signatures of the decision maker and of the outcome
   signer are checked by Section 3 in either case.

4.  Approvals Are Honoured At Most Once

   An approval in Case 1 frees one action once.  For live use, the
   relying party MUST apply the time bounds of Section 3.3 with its own
   current time as T.  A historical audit time MUST NOT make an expired
   approval usable.  A relying party that lets an action run because of
   an approval:




Gruszka                    Expires 6 May 2027                  [Page 23]

Internet-Draft           AUDIT Decision Receipts           November 2026


   1.  MUST honour each approval at most once;

   2.  MUST key its consumption state by the identity of the approval
       (approvalId, actionDigest and session), not by the bytes of one
       copy of the approval record.  Copies or reissues with other
       metadata count as the same approval and MUST NOT extend its
       original validUntil.  Extending that validity requires a new
       approval act and a new approvalId;

   3.  MUST mark the approval as consumed durably before it either
       allows the action to run or releases a signed ALLOW that cites
       the approval;

   4.  MUST treat an approval recorded as consumed in any current,
       rotated, archived or compacted representation as consumed for as
       long as item 6 requires that state to be retained;

   5.  MUST NOT honour an approval unless it can consult a complete,
       durable view of the consumption state required by this section.
       Rotation, archiving or compaction MAY replace earlier generations
       if all entries still required by item 6 are preserved.  Missing
       or unreadable state whose required entries have not been
       preserved elsewhere MUST cause refusal;

   6.  MAY drop an entry only after the approval's original validUntil,
       plus the clock skew it allows, has passed and no copy of that
       approval can still be honoured.

   Rotation is the step where this requirement is most easily lost.  A
   record that is full is moved aside so that new approvals can be
   written; if the relying party then reads only the new generation,
   approvals of the old one may become usable again.  Items 4 and 5
   preserve the consumed state without requiring a particular storage
   format.  A decision receipt for Case 1 binds the approval record by
   digest, so Section 3.5 can expose distinct ALLOW decisions for the
   same approval identity.  Preventing reuse of an approval or of an
   already issued ALLOW remains the relying party's duty.

6.  Relation to Other Work

   [GILDA] defines one in-toto predicate type, signed in DSSE with
   [RFC8785] canonical bytes, that carries an authorization decision and
   the observed effect together with a derived agreement between them,
   and offers it as a data model for AUDIT records.  This document keeps
   decision and outcome in two receipts signed under different keys and
   maps three cases onto the architecture; the consumption-state input
   that [GILDA] names concerns the same kind of state as Section 4.
   [GILDA] lets records carry a sequence number, so that a record
   withheld from a party that holds a later record of the same series
   can be detected (Section 6.3 of [GILDA]); the decision receipts of
   this document carry no such member (Section 3.2).  [GILDA] also
   derives the agreement between decision and effect inside one record
   (Section 5.3 of [GILDA]), where this document compares two receipts
   through decisionRef (Section 3.4).

   [IZMAYLOV] specifies permissions that a principal signs with a
   passkey and action receipts signed by the agent, linked to earlier
   receipts and appended to a log by a recorder.  It also defines a
   passkey-signed approval of one action.  Such an approval could supply
   additional evidence for Case 1.  Citing it by digest does not replace
   the approval record or the checks of Section 3.3; this document
   defines no mapping from that format to the approval record.  In
   [IZMAYLOV] each action receipt carries its place in a chain; every
   receipt after the first also names the preceding receipt by digest.
   It is signed with the agent's keys or, for a helper, with the keys in
   the to member of its delegation (Section 6 of [IZMAYLOV]).  Here the
   decision maker signs a decision receipt and the executor or an
   observer signs an outcome receipt (Section 3.1); a verifier checks
   that executor.id differs from decisionMaker.id (Section 3.4).
   Section 3.2 does not define a chain of decision receipts.

   [WRIGHT] defines a verdict record that states a result on a claim and
   cites its evidence by digest, in any receipt format.  A decision or
   outcome receipt of this document can be such evidence, and a verdict
   record can state the result of the procedure of Section 3.

   [BIRKHOLZ] defines a data format for agent conversation records
   protected by COSE signatures, which [AUDIT-ARCH] names as a candidate
   for Interaction Records.  A signed conversation record can be cited
   by digest as additional input.  Establishing which entry records a
   human approval and how it corresponds to the action, approver,
   session and validity of Case 1 requires a mapping not defined here.
   The approval record and the checks of Section 3.3 still apply.

   SCITT [RFC9943] registers Signed Statements with a Transparency
   Service, which returns Receipts.  Section 3.7 describes how a
   decision or outcome receipt can be registered and what a Receipt then
   shows.

7.  Security Considerations

   Key binding.  A receipt is only as trustworthy as the verifier's
   choice of keys and its policy for the identities those keys may
   attest.  Section 3.2 checks decisionMaker.id; Section 3.4 checks the
   expected executor.id under the outcome-signing key.  An observer's
   signature over an executor identifier is not the executor's own
   signature.  A key held in the recorded Agent's process gives an
   Auditor that does not trust that Agent little assurance.  The
   inspected Agent Guard design uses separate service accounts as
   described in Section 5.

   Key compromise.  Whoever holds the signing key of a decision maker or
   of an outcome signer can produce receipts with any decidedAt or
   performedAt, since these times come from the signer's clock, as the
   paragraph on clocks below says.  A check under Section 3 does not
   tell such a receipt from one signed before the key was compromised or
   replaced.  Which keys are accepted for which identity and for which
   period is set by the verifier's trust policy, not by this document;
   [RFC9943] likewise leaves revocation strategies for compromised keys
   out of its scope (Section 9.4.2 of [RFC9943]).  A time anchor outside
   the signer helps, if it was set before the compromise.  Where the
   envelope bytes are the payload of a registered Signed Statement
   (Section 3.7), a Receipt for it shows that the DSSE signature existed
   when that Signed Statement was registered, and the Transparency
   Service records the Registration time (Section 7 of [RFC9943]).
   Where the Signed Statement is made over the content root, the
   comparison binds the decoded DSSE payload, not the envelope bytes or
   the DSSE signature, and does not establish that the DSSE signature
   existed when the content root was registered (Section 3.7).  The
   rules of Section 4 keep a relying party from honouring one approval
   more than once; they do not prevent the holder of a compromised
   decision maker key from signing receipts that cite approvals with new
   identities.

   Exact bytes.  A verifier that re-serializes a payload before checking
   it can be led to accept bytes that were never signed, or to compute
   another content root.  Step 4 refuses such payloads, and vector
   V2-bad tests it.

   Replay.  An ALLOW for one action must not free another, and an
   outcome that names another decision must not count as the result of
   this one. parametersDigest binds the action bytes, validity binds



Gruszka                    Expires 6 May 2027                  [Page 28]

Internet-Draft           AUDIT Decision Receipts           November 2026


   audience, nonce and expiry, decisionRef binds the outcome, and
   Section 4 requires the relying party to prevent reuse of an approval.
   V1-bad tests rejection of changed payload bytes under an unchanged
   signature.  V3-bad tests rejection of an outcome bound to a different
   decision.  The seven vectors do not test consumption-state retention
   or rotation.

   Self-assertion. actionOutcome in a decision receipt and an outcome
   receipt whose effect cannot be resolved are statements of their
   signers.  A digest that is present but cannot be resolved to bytes
   was chosen by the signer.

   Suppression.  A decision maker can fail to write a receipt for a
   decision it does not want seen.  Independent observers at the
   boundary, as the architecture requires for Action Records, reduce
   this risk.  Registration with a Transparency Service helps address
   selective registration when Relying Parties accept a Signed Statement
   only if they can discover and verify a Receipt for that Signed
   Statement from a Transparency Service they trust (Sections 7.1 and
   9.3 of [RFC9943]).  This document does not ensure that a receipt is
   produced for every decision.  A SCITT Receipt shows inclusion of the
   registered Signed Statement, not that every receipt was registered
   (Section 3.7).  Completeness presupposes an expectation of which
   receipts ought to exist, and this document does not define that
   expectation.

   Gaps.  For outcome receipts, [PB-OUTCOME] defines an optional member
   sequence, with a run identifier and a counter that an executor can
   opt into (its sections 4 and 7); Section 3.4 admits that member.  A
   verifier that holds numbered outcome receipts of a run can report a
   gap in the numbers it has.  Such a gap can reflect withholding or
   delivery delay; the numbers alone do not establish its cause.  Those
   sequence numbers alone do not show whether receipts above the highest
   observed number have been withheld, and a run without the member
   leaves no such trace (compare Section 6.3 of [GILDA]).  The profile
   of Section 3.2 defines no sequence counter or chain of decision
   receipts.  Records that a Service or Tool emits at the boundary where
   an action took effect (Section 4.1 of [AUDIT-ARCH]) can expose an
   effect for which the Auditor has no matching decision receipt.












Gruszka                    Expires 6 May 2027                  [Page 29]

Internet-Draft           AUDIT Decision Receipts           November 2026


   Clocks. decidedAt and performedAt come from the signers' clocks.
   Ordering against outside events needs a timestamp or a transparency
   registration.  A verified time-stamp token under [RFC3161] for a hash
   of the envelope bytes is, like a registration (Section 3.7), an
   anchor outside the signer for a verifier that trusts the time-
   stamping authority.  It supports the assertion that those bytes
   existed no later than genTime plus the applicable accuracy
   (Section 2.4.2 of [RFC3161]).  If the accuracy field is absent, the
   accuracy may be available through other means, such as the TSA
   policy; its absence does not establish zero uncertainty.

8.  Privacy Considerations

   The example receipts in Appendix A carry digests of action bytes,
   approval records and effects rather than those bytes, and the example
   decision receipts set privacy.rawInputsIncluded to false.  The field
   rules of Section 3.8 allow that member to be true and permit raw
   content in other admitted members, such as a decision receipt's open
   traceContext object.  A successful check under Section 3 does not
   establish that raw inputs are absent.  A digest of a short or
   predictable input, such as a command line, can be found by trying
   candidate inputs; a deployment that must hide such inputs keeps the
   receipts as private as the inputs, or adds a secret salt to the
   digested bytes.  Identifiers such as principal.id, agent.id and
   traceContext link a person's actions across services, as Section 9 of
   [AUDIT-ARCH] describes; pairwise or opaque identifiers reduce that
   linkage.  Registering a receipt reveals that a decision took place at
   a time, even when only its content root is registered.  Where the
   envelope bytes are the payload of a registered Signed Statement
   (Section 3.7), readers of that payload can read the receipt with its
   identifiers and digests.  Registering a Statement over the content
   root avoids including the receipt's envelope bytes in that Statement,
   but the Signed Statement still carries its Issuer and Subject Claims
   and the Transparency Service records the Registration time (Sections
   6 and 7 of [RFC9943]).  Access to the registered data and any
   detached payload depends on the Transparency Service and any Adjacent
   Services; Issuers and Clients assess their privacy and security
   posture before registration (Section 8 of [RFC9943]).

A.2.  Statements

   The payload bytes of each vector are the serialization under
   [RFC8785] of a Statement {"_type": "https://in-toto.io/Statement/v1",
   "predicateType": P, "predicate": D, "subject": [{"name": N, "digest":
   {"sha256": H}}]}, where H is SHA-256 over the serialization of D.
   For a decision receipt P is the decision receipt type and N is
   "decision:" followed by decisionId; for an outcome receipt P is the
   outcome receipt type and N is "outcome:" followed by outcomeId.

   Each predicate is given as a list of assignments that starts from the
   predicate named in its first line, {} being the empty object.  An
   assignment sets the member at the path to the JSON value shown.  A
   path is a sequence of member names joined by dots, where a decimal
   index i in square brackets after a name selects element i of an
   array; missing objects on the path are created, and an assignment to
   element i of an array with i elements appends it.  Members not named
   keep their value.  SHA-256(x) stands for the JSON string of the 64
   lowercase hexadecimal digits of the SHA-256 of input x, and R(V) for
   that of the payload bytes of vector V.

B.1.  Changes from -03

   New Appendix A.4 gives one example Signed Statement for each way of
   registration in Section 3.7, signed with a new test key, and a SCITT
   Receipt for each, generated with a library for illustration, and
   Section 3.7 points to it.  The informative references add RFC 8949,
   RFC 9052, RFC 9053, RFC 9162, RFC 9597, RFC 9679, RFC 9942, RFC 9995
   and scitt-cose.

   Section 3.2 step 7 and Section 3.4 step 3 list by full path the
   members that are digests and timestamps, and step 7 gives the form of
   evidenceRefs[].typedDigest.  Appendix A.3 names the procedure that
   decides each vector.  Appendix A.4 states that the entry of the tree
   is the choice of the example.  New Appendix A.5 gives failure vectors
   for steps 7 to 10 of Section 3.2, Case 1 of Section 3.3, steps 5 to 7
   of Section 3.4 and Section 4.

   New Section 3.8 gives the field rules of both predicates as tables,
   step 7 of Section 3.2 and step 3 of Section 3.4 refer to it, and
   [PB-DECISION] and [PB-OUTCOME] are informative references.  The field
   rules distinguish JSON booleans from string literals.  Step 1 of
   Section 3.2 rejects duplicate member names in DSSE envelopes.
   Appendix A.5 identifies the first failing check for each stated test
   procedure, distinguishes the relying-party check for F10, and
   explains that F9 violates the Case 1 time-bound condition
   independently of T.  Section 5 and Appendix A distinguish the
   provenance of the seven DSSE vectors from that of the new examples.
   Section 8 clarifies that the field rules do not establish the absence
   of raw inputs.  Case 1 of Section 3.3 allows inputSnapshot entries
   other than the approval record, and Section 3.6 states which digests
   a pass checks against bytes.
